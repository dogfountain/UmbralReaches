# The Umbral Reaches: the mythos bible

The valley's story, for anyone writing an inscription, a toast, a boss line or an item text. Every
readable text in the world lives in `core/world/realm/lore_db.gd`; this file is the canon behind it.
House style: terse, concrete, dark. Two sentences beat three. Nobody explains the world to the
player; they complain about it, mourn it, or carve a warning into it.

## The shape of it in one breath

The Order of the Open Sky taught that the sky was a door and the faithful could climb through it.
They built the Stair on the mountain, filled the valley with pilgrims, and on the Night of the
Open Door the Hierarch Aurel performed the Ascension at the summit. The sky opened: but inward,
like water down a drain. It drank the pilgrims on the Stair, tore the land open where the Stair
stood (the Ashen Rift), and kept drinking. The light has been leaking out of the valley ever since.
That cold is **the gloom**. A smith's widow, Edda, lit a fire on the bare altar of a wayside chapel
and the gloom would not cross it. That was **the First Kindling**. Every brazier the wanderer lights
is her refusal, repeated.

## Powers and people

- **The sky turning inward.** Not a storm; a wound. The vortex over the Crowns is the Ascension
  still in progress: the door was never closed, because the priest who opened it is still holding
  it. While it turns, light and warmth drain out of the valley.
- **The gloom.** The cold dark the open sky pulls down. It drinks warmth first, then will, then
  the self. Firelight is the only thing it will not cross. (Gameplay: the gloom meter; braziers
  and lit places drain it.)
- **The hollowed.** People the gloom emptied. Not dead: *emptied*. What walks afterwards is a
  **shade**. When they come apart they leave **grave ash**, the last of their warmth, and fires
  burn brighter for it ("Ash to ember, ember to flame"). The wanderer, too, can be hollowed and
  walk back from a fire.
- **Who comes back down.** One rule, said once: those who went all the way through the door are
  gone and do not come back. Those the door half-took come down **hollow** (Hobb, Wenna's mother,
  Father Harrow's kind). Once the door is shut the hollow ones can lie down, and the half-hollow
  who kept a little warmth (Pell) can walk home. "What climbs comes down hollow" is the Rooted's
  version of the same rule.
- **The wanderer.** A late pilgrim, like the Ninth Company: climbed what was left of the Stair, and
  the door half-took them. They came down not quite hollow: a void where the face was, and a
  splinter of the open sky in the violet orb (the vortex's colour, and the Warden's eye). That is
  why the wanderer's powers are the sky's (Rift Bolt, Umbral Nova, Rift Step) and why they walk
  back from the fire. Aurel's third edict ("until the last of you is through") was held, at the
  end, for them; a later hand on the rim stele adds "One is still on the Stair." Seed it; never
  explain it: Nan ("You haven't got a face under there. That's all right."), Abel ("You come back
  the way they do. Only you come back warm."), the keep's third answer ("He was waiting for me.").
- **Saint Edda the Kindler.** Hearthward's smith, a widow. Not holy, by her own account: "a smith
  who would not look up." Lit the First Hearth. Buried in the chapel yard under a plain stone.
- **Saint Corvin.** A brother of the Order who carried fire to the fen burials so the sunk dead
  would have light. The penitents took his face and his hands.
- **Wenna Ashby.** A pilgrim girl from the western road whose journal pages are scattered from
  the West Gate to the fen. Her mother went up the Stair. Wenna did not.
- **Old Maud.** Keeps one candle in her window so her son Hobb will see it from the sky. Writes him
  letters she never sends. Something knocks at her door at night, three slow knocks, as Hobb used
  to; she does not open.
- **The bells.** (r39 canon: the rim's three are the Order's, cast first; the Founder's **peal** is eight bells for the roads, seven for the far countries and the eighth for the Marches, which the Order shut out. One opens the door; each more weakens the one who holds it.) The **Founder**, Brannoc's mother, cast the rim's three great bells (Hope,
  Patience, Homecoming) in a furnace under the rim. **Brannoc** cast the forty small bells of the
  Stair, and the Hierarch's great bell at the summit. On the Night they stopped **top first**. Rung
  right they go **bottom to top**, the great bell first ("Mother's order"): that is the rim peal, and
  the one bell at the ending. The Deeper Night's bell is cracked and hung mouth up, and rings **top
  first**, the wrong way. The explore music keeps no bell until the ending has rung.
- **Brother Lisle.** The lay brother who kept **the Tally**, the Abbot's count of the waiting dead.
  The count began to go *up*. He told the Abbot, who was glad. Lisle went down to count once more
  and was added to it.
- **The Hierarch's three edicts.** I: every soul on the road is the sky's, lanterns are for doubters.
  II: on the Night, no fire below the Stair (this is why the villages were dark when the gloom came).
  III, cut the morning of the Night: "I will hold the door until the last of you is through." He is
  still holding it; that is the Ascended Warden. (Under it, in another hand: "One is still on the Stair.")
  **Staged reveal**: the keep and Brannoc know *what* holds the door, not *who*, until the player has
  a boss down (Brannoc: "If that's him up there, I cast it for nothing."). Edict III confirms; the
  boss bar renames itself **AUREL, HIERARCH** in phase III.
- **The keep of the Kindled Keg.** A dwarf who serves the kindled and not the unlit. Fire stays;
  hollows do not. The Keg's folk (the keep and every patron) are dwarves, the last of the Rooted's
  kin, who were in the valley before the road and never looked up: their cellar is older than the
  Stair, Hesk's grandmother's people raised the Ring. Hearthward's villagers are human.

## The clause the scribe cut (r39)

Edict III as dictated, at dawn on the Night, to Perrin Aldwyn of the third desk: *I will hold the door until the last of you is
through, and then I will follow.* Perrin cut the first half; the second sounded like a threat and he was afraid. Aurel was not holding
the door to trap them. He was holding it so they could pass, and meant to shut it behind them and go after. The wanderer, the last
through, can cut the clause back in, leave the stone, or scratch it out (quest The Third Edict), and Aurel answers each differently.
The Ninth Company's youngest, **Ione Marsh** (the tenth name nobody finished), is in the Glimmer Glade; the Tally's last line,
*ONE LATE PILGRIM, NO NAME*, is the wanderer. Other new people: **Odile Frane** (Ned's widow; three hundred and eleven road lamps),
**Cantor Lowe** and the boy **Tavi** in the horn, **Tamsin Hale** the baker who feeds the Anchoress's cell.

## The Late Ringer, and what the new readables fix (r39 second wave)

**Iorweth**, the last ringer of the Stair, rang the last bell and the longest ("rang last and longest"; his name is cut short on the stone at the Crossing's foot and
finished with a nail). The door half-took him: he came down hollow, then warm, as the wanderer did, and has walked toward a bell ever since, with his rope still round his
waist. He is **Ione Marsh's father** (the tenth name nobody finished: I-O-N). Once the door is shut a half-hollow man may walk home, and he does. The **Hierarch's Hand** (six leaves),
the **Founder's Ledger** (eight, one at each bell's country) and the **scratches on the road** (six) are readable through the world; the landmarks each have their own: the Sleeper
(the Order's first colossus, pushed on the first night), the Hollow King (the Rooted's kings' tomb, taken for a chapel), the Salt Ship (the merchantman Hope-in-Season, a mile a winter), the Wardens'
Line (ordered by Edict II to put out their cressets), the Chain Span (toll: one lantern-light), the Titan Gates (the Rooted's work; the Order gave them helms).

## The three who hold the valley (bosses)

- **The Thornwarden** (Thorn Henge, the Ring of Thorns, on the western hill in the Thornwood).
  Older than the ascension; the Rooted bound it into the stones to keep the oaks. In speech it is
  always **the Thornwarden** (or "the keeper" on the Rooted's stones), never "the Warden". When the abbey
  came with axes it woke, and it has never fully slept since. It does not serve the sky or the
  fire; it keeps the wood. Its **Thorn Heart** still beats, slowly. Cut it out and the wood will
  bleed, and stop bleeding, and forget you.
- **The Bone Abbot** (the Ossuary, the necropolis's charnel house: a quarried bone pit under the Waiting Field's
  slope, reached by a stone bridge from the Pilgrims' Landing, west of the First Pilgrims' Tomb). Abbot Osric, who kept the dead in order for their ascension and never stopped counting
  them. He calls the faithful from the bones. His **crown** is knucklebone and iron; the **Bone
  Reliquary** rises from his charnel mound when he falls.
- **The Ascended Warden** (the summit, across the Crossing). Once the Hierarch Aurel, last priest
  of the Ascension. He went halfway up the sky and stopped: hollow, towering, still holding the door
  open. His sigil, the **Ascension Seal**, is the door's latch; it falls out of his opened fingers.
  In his fight he holds the door with one hand (phase II: "One hand never leaves the door.") and
  lets go with it in phase III ("He lets go of the door with one hand.").

The **summit ward** answers only to the Thorn Heart and the Abbot's Crown: the valley's two powers
of *below*. Carved on it: "Let none climb to the door but by the leave of the two who keep the
valley down: the root, and the count. A." When one is missing it says which ("Nothing answers.
The crypt's crown still holds below.").

Boss subtitles: the Thornwarden *what the Rooted asked to stand*; the Bone Abbot *Abbot Osric,
still counting*; the Ascended Warden *the one who holds the door*.

## The ending and the Deeper Night

- **The ending** is one beat (story.gd): the Warden's bar drops; no toasts; 1.5 s of silence while
  the sky's spin eases to **zero** (reactivity.gd, ~9 s; the twist stays); "The sky stops turning.";
  one deep bell, once, everywhere; "Somewhere above the Crowns, a bell rings once. From the bottom
  up." / "The cold stays. It no longer pulls." The keep's last talk has three answers to "Aurel?"
  (let go / hollow / "He was waiting for me."), the rule of who comes back down, and one line from
  what the player did (the lantern, the pilgrim who went home, Nan's mug).
- **The Deeper Night** (NG+): the cracked bell rung top first; "Something holds the door again."
  The story files this cycle's ending (`ending_*` -> `ending_*_ng<n>`) and sets `deeper`, so the
  next Warden gets his own narration, talk and card; the keep greets it ("You rang it. ...Wrong way
  up."), and Hesk, Ostrin and Brannoc each notice.

## Recurring motifs (use them, don't explain them)

- **Up vs. down.** The Order looks up; the Rooted bury face down; Edda "would not look up".
  Fire holds you below. Hollowed things came down from somewhere.
- **Bells** stopped from the top down; rung right, from the bottom up. Chalked messages promise bells that never come.
- **Three stones** for the fen dead: eyes, mouth, heart.
- **Doors**: the open door in the sky; "a cold hearth is an open door"; "we kept the door open";
  sealed barrows "until the sky calls".
- **Counting**: waystones count miles up to the Stair, the Order counts years up from its coming,
  the Abbot counts his dead.
- **Faces**: scraped saints, the wanderer's dark void face, the hollowed have none worth keeping.
- **Three knocks**: what comes back knocks three times, as it did when it was someone.
- **The open door** is the Order's sign (carved over its edicts) and the glade's answer to it
  ("we kept the door open").
